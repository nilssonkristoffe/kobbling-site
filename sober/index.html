How it works

Three steps. One of them takes ten seconds.

1

Pick a break or stop

A break has an end date, like 30 days. Stopping has none. You can extend whenever you like.

2

One tap a day

How was today? Rest your finger on the lantern. The reminder never gives away what it's about.

3

Watch your island grow

Every day you make it adds a piece: the jetty, the shed, the lighthouse, the boat. And the guests find their way.

When a craving hits

A craving is like a wave. It rises, peaks and settles.

Tap the wave and you ride it out together. Breathe for one, three or five minutes, get up and change rooms, or call someone you chose ahead of time.

The helplines are always one tap away.

Hard days

You know which days will be hard. Now you can be ready.

The Friday dinner, payday weekend, the wedding. Add a storm warning, decide ahead of time what you'll do when the wind picks up, and get your plan in the morning.

We're here when it's hardest, too.

New Year's Eve, Midsummer, Christmas: the evenings that tend to be the hardest. The island dresses up, and the day before it asks if you'd like a plan. Help with cravings is one tap away, day and night.

A living island

Morning, day and evening. Your island comes along.

The sky shifts with the time of day, the island dresses for the season, and if you like, it can have the same weather as where you are.

Every time you open the app it looks a little different. Just like a real island.

A bad day

One bad day doesn't erase the good ones.

Didn't go as planned today? Answer honestly. The lantern goes out and your streak starts over, but your island keeps every piece and your badges stay.

Coming back the day after is the hardest step. So it gets a badge of its own.

The island

Your island grows with you, and guests find their way.

Kobben has sat on this rock for a thousand years. He's patient, but he notices every piece. In time, new guests find their way, from Ärla, who comes the morning after your first day, to the seasonal visitors.

Every guest has a name and a little story of their own, and they all end up in the guestbook.

There. Now the island is a little more yours.
